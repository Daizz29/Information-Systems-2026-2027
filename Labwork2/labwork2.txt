Notation:
  σ  selection (WHERE)        π  projection (SELECT columns)
  ⋈  natural join             ρ  rename
  ∧  and                      ≠  not equal

Note: dept_emp, salaries and dept_manager all have the columns
from_date and to_date. A natural join between two of them would also
match on these dates, so the date columns are projected away first.


1. All info of all employees

   employees


2. All info of all departments

   departments


3. Full names of all employees

   π first_name, last_name (employees)


4. Names of all departments

   π dept_name (departments)


5. Full names of employees working in "ICT" department

   π first_name, last_name ( employees ⋈ dept_emp ⋈ σ dept_name='ICT' (departments) )


6. Full names of male employees working in "BIO" department

   π first_name, last_name ( σ gender='M' (employees) ⋈ dept_emp ⋈ σ dept_name='BIO' (departments) )


7. Salaries of female employees working in "WEO" department

   π first_name, last_name, salary (
         σ gender='F' (employees)
       ⋈ π emp_no, salary (salaries)
       ⋈ π emp_no, dept_no (dept_emp)
       ⋈ σ dept_name='WEO' (departments) )


8. Full names of employees who have the same last name as their manager

   E ← π emp_no, first_name, last_name, dept_no ( employees ⋈ dept_emp )

   M ← ρ M(m_emp_no, m_last_name, dept_no) (
           π emp_no, last_name, dept_no ( employees ⋈ dept_manager ) )

   Result ← π first_name, last_name (
               σ last_name = m_last_name ∧ emp_no ≠ m_emp_no ( E ⋈ M ) )

   (E ⋈ M joins on dept_no: each employee is paired with the managers
    of his/her department.)


9. Full names of employees who was paid more than $100000

   π first_name, last_name ( employees ⋈ σ salary>100000 (salaries) )


10. Names of all departments that have employees paid more than $1000000

   π dept_name (
         departments
       ⋈ π emp_no, dept_no (dept_emp)
       ⋈ π emp_no ( σ salary>1000000 (salaries) ) )
